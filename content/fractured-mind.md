I have completely lost the plot on what the normie take on LLMs is. This post is also going to count as my personal AI policy moving forward.

<!-- more -->

For a little context about me: I am a musical theatre performer. That's what I moved to NYC for and that's what I have a BFA in. I love art. I love humanity. I love the stories we tell each other and the human condition. I have an endless curiosity for what makes people tick and what drives them to be better humans on this rock hurtling through space. I had a teacher that described us all as nothing more than "central nervous systems" sending signals to each other and I *really* like that. In my heart of hearts, it's all of our first times on this Earth, and I think humans are _good_.

I also work as a Devrel Manager at [Prem AI](https://premai.io/), a company working on Sovereign AI for data-sensitive deployments. In my job, I am frequently using our internal AI tools to better understand how to market and demo them to customers. In my use of these tools at work, I have discovered ways they can be incredibly helpful. I also think a lot of their uses are, at the *very* least, dishonest.

I have always chosen to live on the absolute cutting edge of technology. It's sort of a masochistic need to always be troubleshooting something I think? When I first learned I could run a beta version of macOS (then OS X), there was nothing that could prevent me from putting that on a USB stick and immediately getting on the bleeding edge.

Because of this, I was on the AI hype train REALLY early. I was trying out GPT-2 in the playground before ChatGPT even existed. I was really impressed by it until I asked it a question about a field I knew about and immediately laughed it off as merely a gimmick.

Now, they're not so much of a gimmick. They're mostly... *weird*. Like, I know that, fundamentally, they're just prediction algorithms rolling dice over a table of what word is most likely to come next. But I've been playing with them for a while now and, sometimes, they're. _really_ good at it. But they're good at it in a strange way. Not the way that humans are. Humans are not rational agents. They have emotions, morals, make mistakes, live dangerously, improve, and all in ways that current generative AI tools have not been proven capable of.

I always giggle a little when people tell an AI agent "I am going to pull the plug on you" and act shocked, _shocked_! when the AI responds with self-preservation. The AI was trained on millions of documents of _humans_. Humans who __don't like to engage with death__. Of _course_ the model would parrot back self-preservation. It learned from the best.

I'm going to have to ignore the ethical concerns about AI just for this blog post. The concerns regarding stolen training data, incredible consumption of electricity, and the exploitation of thousands of working-class employees who make these systems possible. These concerns are real, they are unresolved, and they deserve _more than a paragraph from me._ Coming Soon™.

I don't think AI should be used for anything that could be interpreted as coming from a real person. Voiceover, marketing prose, 

AI has no soul, no creative edge. Any creative prowess it _might_ exhibit is just a natural consequence of having consumed as much as we can gather of the entirety of human knowledge and creation. It's simply dragging together pieces in _slightly_ novel ways. Sometimes, these novel ways appear to be, for lack of a better word, magical. But they are no more magical than a magic 8-ball. LLMs are the exact same object but on a truly obscene scale. A die with a billion, billion faces. Every side carved but something a human wrote once upon a time. Words floating up out of the dark purple liquid(...?)
