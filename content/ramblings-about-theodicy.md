From my childhood, I had a pretty weird relationship with religion. I grew up pushed away from spirituality and divinity and much more towards nihilism.

<!-- more -->

This nihilism, for a long time, really brought me down. I didn’t really believe in the sanctity of anything, I guess? I believed that it was a random roll of the cosmic dice that brought us here. 

Because of this upbringing, I was left with a lot of disparate thoughts about God. I know a lot of people with religious trauma that also have a lot of these, but somehow, by not having any religious education at all, I managed to just exist in this gray area. A no-man's land where I didn't feel like I belonged in discussions about theology or on r/atheism. I abhorred the people I saw using religion as a tool to aid exclusion, but saw atheists doing the exact same thing so naturally pulled away from both camps.

My first mandatory run in with religion was a theology class during the first year of my BFA program at Molloy University, a Catholic institution. I *dreaded* this class.

That is, until I met my teacher. This genuine angel of a human changed my whole outlook on religion, what it can be, what it can do for people, and God. My first "holy shit" moment in his class came when we were discussing depictions of divinity. I’ll remember this forever because it truly short circuited me: “the problem with depicting God is that as time goes on, the depictions tend to resemble the ruling class.”

This almost single-handedly threw every preconceived notion I had about religion into immediate question. Over the years and the multiple classes I took with him, my thoughts about religion began to crystallize. What I found is that basically all of my gripes with religion tend to disappear as soon as you start visualizing God as a sort of one-ness with the universe. An all powerful energy that exists inside of every human. 

God must not ever be anthropomorphized. To assign human traits to God is to diminish their power. Because inherently any visualization of a God with any human traits immediately dismisses the unknowable infinite God should possess.
